# LinkedIn Launch Post

The verbatim launch post for the AI-Securing Threat Hunt Program.

---

AI security cannot stay trapped in policy decks and prompt-injection hot takes.

I just built the foundation for an AI-Securing Threat Hunt Program — a practical operating model for finding risk across the systems companies are already rushing to deploy.

The problem is not just "Is the model safe?"

The real questions are:
What AI assets exist?
Which tools can act on behalf of users?
What data can prompts, agents, RAG pipelines, plugins, and vector stores access?
Where are logs missing?
What does abnormal behavior look like when the threat surface includes humans, models, APIs, identity, and automation?
Can the SOC detect misuse before AI turns a normal workflow into a blast-radius event?

AI governance without threat hunting is paperwork.
AI threat hunting without governance becomes chaos.

The program I'm building connects both: asset inventory, telemetry, hunt hypotheses, detections, response playbooks, hunter guardrails, and executive metrics.

This is blue-team AI security in the real world: not fear, not hype, not "buy a tool and pray."

Evidence. Controls. Hunts. Response.

I'm turning this into a fast-track series of deliverables and likely a field guide/book because security teams need something more practical than another AI risk acronym.

If your organization is deploying AI and your detection program still assumes the attack surface stops at endpoint, network, cloud, and identity, your threat model is already behind.
